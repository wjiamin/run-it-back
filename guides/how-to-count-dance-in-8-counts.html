How to count music in 8-counts for dance

Dancers count music in groups of eight: 1, 2, 3, 4, 5, 6, 7, 8, then 1 again. It sounds basic, but once you can hear the 8-counts in a song, learning choreography gets much easier.

What an 8-count is

Almost all pop music is built on a steady beat grouped in fours. Two groups of four make an 8-count, which is roughly one musical phrase. Choreographers build moves on this grid, so dance teachers say things like "the arm wave is on 3" or "we turn on 7-8".

The spaces between numbers are called "and": "1 and 2 and 3…". Fast moves often land on the "and".

How to find the 1

  1. Tap along to the beat first, without numbers. Tap to the drum pulse you'd naturally nod your head to.
  2. Listen for where things start. A new section (verse, chorus), the first word of a line, or a big hit or drop usually lands on a 1.
  3. Count 1 to 8 from there and check that the next section also starts on a 1. If it lands on a 5 instead, you started counting halfway through: move your 1 by four beats.
  4. Watch the dancers. Big moves and formation changes tend to hit the 1.

Many K-pop songs sit around 90 to 130 beats per minute, so each 8-count lasts about 4 to 5 seconds.

Why counting speeds up learning

  • You learn in chunks. "The chorus is four 8-counts" is far easier to remember than one long stream of moves.
  • You can find your place. Stuck? You know it's "the third 8 of the chorus", and you can loop just that.
  • You stay on the beat. Counting "5, 6, 7, 8" out loud before you start sets you up to hit the 1 on time.
