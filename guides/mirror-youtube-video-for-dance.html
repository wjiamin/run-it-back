How to mirror a YouTube video for dance practice

When you face a dancer on screen, their right hand is on your left. Your brain has to flip every move before you can copy it. A mirrored video does the flip for you, so learning it feels like following a mirror in a studio.

Why mirroring helps

  • No mental flipping. When they step to the screen's right, you step right.
  • Fewer wrong-side habits. Learning a move on the wrong side, then relearning it, costs far more time than learning it right once.
  • It's how dance classes work. In a studio you follow the teacher's reflection in the mirror. A mirrored video looks the same way.

The exception: when a video is already filmed from behind the dancers, don't mirror it.

Option 1: search for a mirrored upload

Search YouTube for the song name plus "dance practice mirrored". Popular songs usually have one. The catch: they're re-uploads, sometimes lower quality, sometimes cropped, sometimes taken down, and newer or less popular songs often don't have one at all.

Option 2: a browser extension

Some browser extensions add a mirror button to the YouTube player. They work on a computer, but not in phone browsers, and each one asks for permission to read the pages you visit.

Tips for learning from a mirrored video

  • Stand so the screen is in front of you, like a studio mirror, rather than off to the side.
  • When a formation changes, follow your member's position on screen: if they move screen-left, you move left.
  • Film yourself facing the same way, and compare the mirrored video with your own recording side by side.
